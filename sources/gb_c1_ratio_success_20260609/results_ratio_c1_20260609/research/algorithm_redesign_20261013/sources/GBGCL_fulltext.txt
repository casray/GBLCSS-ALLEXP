Multi-Granularity Graph Contrastive Learning
Framework via Granular-Ball on Heterogeneous
Graphs
Shuyin Xia, Guan Wang, Cheng Tan, Chao Huang, Sen Zhao ∗, Yi Liu, Yi Wang, Wei Wang, Guoyin Wang,
Xinbo Gao
Abstract—Graph Contrastive Learning (GCL) is a popular
self-supervised learning (SSL) technique. However, mainstream
GCL methods usually favor single fine-grained random aug-
mentation schemes, which will destroy the structural integrity
of the graph, and they largely ignore the topology of the
graph structure, that is, multi-granularity characteristics. Graphs
are typically composed of homogeneous regions with varying
granularities, where nodes within a region exhibit strong ho-
mogeneous properties. However, most of the real graphs are
heterogeneous, and in the local regions of heterogeneous graphs,
interconnected nodes may have similar semantic information,
even if they do not belong to the same class. To this end,
we propose a new multi-granularity graph contrastive learning
framework via granular-ball (GBGCL) to explore the potential
on heterogeneous graphs. Specifically, we develop an adap-
tive granular-ball augmentation strategy that identifies multi-
granularity homogeneous regions in the heterogeneous graph,
and treat nodes in the same granular-ball as positive pairs, while
nodes in different granular-balls as negative pairs. In addition, we
integrate the feature information of the nodes in original graph
and semantic information of similar nodes in the feature space.
Node representations are obtained through joint optimization
of losses. Experiments on heterogeneous graphs demonstrate
the unique advantages of our framework. Code is available at
https://anonymous.4open.science/r/GBGCL/.
Index Terms —Graph contrastive learning, Self-supervised
learning, Heterogeneous graphs, Multi-granularity characteris-
tics.

Homogeneity is an important inductive bias on real-world
graphs and is widely considered to be an important factor in
the success of GNNs [27]. In GNNs, many models are built
upon the homophily assumption, which suggests that nodes
with similar features or identical class labels tend to be con-
nected. However, real-world graphs often exhibit heterophily,
where connected nodes may have dissimilar features or belong
to different categories. Therefore, applying messaging mech-
anisms directly to heterogeneous graphs can pose a problem,
where aggregation of information from different neighbors
can significantly reduce the accuracy of node representations
and ultimately affect the model’s performance on downstream
tasks. In addition, graphs are composed of homogeneous
subregions with different levels of granularity, and the multi-
granularity feature of heterogeneous graphs refers to local
regions with different topologies, and the interconnected nodes
in these regions may have similar semantic information even
if they belong to different classes. GCL usually constructs
positive and negative pairs by graph augmentation methods,
and then enhances the node representation by making the
representation between positive pairs closer and the represen-
tation between negative pairs farther. As shown in the left
half of Fig.1, most existing GCL methods use random graph
augmentation to generate different views, such as edge or
node augmentation based on the finest granularity. However,
these graph augmentation methods often require manual trial
and error, extensive searching, or costly domain knowledge,
and more importantly, the multi-granularity characteristics of
heterogeneous graphs have not been fully explored yet.

In light of this, three challenges come to this paper: (1) The
first challenge is to refine homogeneous structure.the homoge-
neous structure consists of nodes with high connectivity and
strong homogeneity. Therefore, these regions should exhibit
greater homogeneity than adjacent regions, ensuring that nodes
in the region are more similar to each other than nodes outside
the region. In addition, the granularity of homogeneous struc-
tures may vary between nodes. Inspired by the advantage of
granular-ball computing [28] in distinguishing homogeneous
points for scatter data, we further explore its potential for
graph refinement and propose an adaptive granular-ball graph
refinement mechanism to capture homogeneous structures
within the graph. (2) The second challenge is to get augmented
views to build positive and negative pairs. GCL generally
maximizes the similarity between two augmentations (positive
pairs) of one sample and minimizes the similarity between
other samples (negative pairs). Therefore, it is crucial to build
accurate positive and negative pairs, but it can be distorted
by heterogeneous edges, which introduces noise into the node
representation. For this reason, an effective graph augmenta-
tion strategy is needed to identify and retain the homogeneous
structure while reducing the influence of the heterogeneous
edge. (3) The third challenge is joint optimization. Besides
constructing contrastive views, it is also necessary to uncover
the deeper semantic information embedded in the graph data
and the similarities reflected in the feature space. Therefore,
joint optimization is required to obtain a higher quality node
representation.
To address these challenges, we propose GBGCL, a multi-
granularity graph contrastive learning framework via granular-
ball computing on heterogeneous graphs. Specifically, (1) in-
stead of random augmentation, GBGCL introduces an adaptive
granular-ball graph refinement mechanism to capture multi-
granularity homogeneous structures by iteratively decompos-
ing the graph from coarse to fine. (2) For each node, the GBA
strategy performs adaptive augmentation by treating nodes
within the same granular-ball as positive pairs and nodes
from different granular-balls as negative pairs. (3) GBGCL
further integrates original node features with latent semantic
information. The Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) [29] is used
to obtain soft clustering assignments, where node similarity
is estimated according to the probability that neighboring
nodes form positive samples. By jointly optimizing multiple
losses, GBGCL learns high-quality node representations for
downstream tasks. Extensive experiments on heterogeneous
graph datasets demonstrate that GBGCL outperforms baselines
and state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods. Our contributions are
summarized as follows:
• Inspired by the cognitive law of “global precedence” [30],
we propose a novel granular-ball augmentation strategy
(GBA). This strategy can adaptively refine the homo-
geneous structure in the graph, and construct positive
and negative pairs through multi-granularity refinement
results.
• In order to explore the potential of GCL in heterogeneous
graphs, we propose a new multi-granularity graph con-
trastive learning framework via granular-ball on hetero-
geneous graphs (GBGCL), which integrates the structure
and semantic of heterogeneous graphs to obtain effective
node representations.
• Experiments on several heterogeneous datasets show that
our proposed approach achieves performance over SOTA
methods on downstream tasks compared to supervised
and unsupervised baselines.
II. R ELATED WORK
A. Graph contrastive learning
Unlike traditional graph learning methods that rely on
labeled data, GCL uses the graph’s inherent structure to
create contrastive tasks, enabling unsupervised learning of
node, edge, subgraph, or graph-level representations. This
approach is highly effective for various graph-related tasks
without extensive labeled data. Node-level GCL learns node
representations through contrastive learning, often using neigh-
bor information. For example, You et al. applied structural
perturbation for contrastive node representation [21], while
Sun et al. minimized contrastive loss between positive and
negative samples [31]. Edge-level GCL focuses on learning
edge representations to capture node relationships and graph
structure. Zhao et al. proposed an edge prediction task to
enhance structural learning [32]. Subgraph-based GCL com-
pares subgraphs extracted from the graph, optimizing graph
representations for local structure learning. Hamilton et al.
used graph convolution to sample neighborhoods, generate
subgraphs, and perform contrastive learning [33]. Graph-level
GCL treats the entire graph as a unit for representation
learning. For example, You et al. incorporated graph-level
contrastive loss [21].
Among the various variants of GCL, graph augmentation
plays a crucial role in graph representation learning. Conse-
quently, numerous studies have focused on designing effective
graph augmentation strategies tailored to GCL. The early GCL
methods were mainly based on random graph augmentation
methods. For example, GRACE [34] took the lead in using
random edge deletion and feature mask to achieve graph
augmentation. Subsequently, GraphCL [21] explores a variety
of graph augmentation combinations, including randomly node
dropping, edge perturbation, subgraph sampling, and feature
masking. To increase the flexibility of GraphCL, JOAO [35]
also implements automatic selection of the ideal augmentation
strategy for each dataset.
GCL has become a powerful tool for improving graph
representations in unsupervised settings, particularly for het-
erogeneous graphs where labels are scarce. Traditional embed-
ding methods, such as random walks or link prediction, often
struggle to capture the nuanced relationships between diverse
node types [36]. To address these limitations, innovative
approaches have incorporated contrastive mechanisms, often
based on meta-path predictions, to better utilize structures of
heterogeneous graphs. For instance, HeCo [37] enhances node
representations through multi-view learning, GREET [38]
leverages non-homogeneous edges to capture node-specific
features, HLCL [39] combines high- and low-frequency fil-
tering with contrastive learning, and GASSER [40] integrates

B. Granular-ball computing
Chen [30] pointed out that human cognition has the law of
“global precedence” in his research published in Sciences. This
characteristic distinguishes human cognition from existing
artificial intelligence algorithms, which typically focus on
analyzing fine-grained details. The human visual system is
particularly sensitive to the law, which is from coarse-grained
to fine-grained, so directly processing the most fine-grained
raw data is not in line with the human cognitive law. Granular
computing establishes a “coarse-fine” hierarchy for a set of
problem spaces through the use of granularity measures.
Wang [45] was the first to incorporate the “global prece-
dence” cognitive mechanism into granular computing, in-
troducing the idea of multi-granularity cognitive computing.
Expanding on this foundation, Xia and Wang et al. [28]
proposed a new, efficient, and robust granularity computing
method, namely granular-ball computing. The core idea of
this method is to first process the entire data as a coarsest
granular-ball, and then split it into some fine-grained granular-
balls. In granular-ball computing, granular-balls of different
sizes are used to represent the sample space, and subse-
quent calculations are performed based on these granular-balls.
Granular-ball computing has been widely extended to various
artificial intelligence tasks, including rough sets [46]–[48],
clustering [49]–[52], reinforcement learning [53], and graph
neural networks [54]–[58].

IV. M ETHODOLOGY
A detailed description of the proposed multi-granularity
graph contrastive learning framework via granular-ball on
heterogeneous graphs (GBGCL) is then presented. Fig. 2
illustrates the overall framework of GBGCL, which primarily
comprises three modules : (1) The Adaptive Granular-Ball
Graph Refinement . We propose an adaptive Granular-Ball
Augmentation (GBA) strategy that adaptively identifies ho-
mogeneous regions of different granularities in heterogeneous
graphs by splitting granular-balls from coarse to fine. (2) The
Multi-Granularity Positive and Negative Pairs . Nodes in the
same granular-ball are regarded as positive pairs, while nodes
in different granular-balls are regarded as negative pairs. (3)
The Joint Optimization . The original graph and perturbation
graph are sent to the same MLP encoder to learn the represen-
tation. At the same time, the Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM
[29]) is used to perform soft clustering assignment for each
node in the original graph. Finally, the joint optimization of the
three losses is carried out to optimize the node representation.
Next, we introduce each module in detail.
A. Adaptive Granular-Ball Graph Refinement
GCL generally follows the paradigm of “augmentation-
encoding-contrast”, so we first augment the original graph to
build contrastive graph views. On the one hand, most of the
existing random augmentation methods destroy the structural
integrity of the original graph. Due to the preference of high-
frequency connections in heterogeneous graphs, structural per-
turbations may affect the middle- and high-frequency compo-
nents of the original graph [42]. On the other hand, most of the
existing random augmentation methods focus on single fine-
grained, so they largely ignore the multi-granularity features
of graphs. In other words, graphs are usually composed of
homogeneous regions with different granularity, and the nodes
within these regions exhibit strongly homogeneous properties.
Therefore, random augmentation is not adopted in this
paper, in order to preserve the structural information of graphs
and better exploit the multi-granularity characteristics of het-
erogeneous graphs. Inspired by the granular-ball computing
[28], we propose the GBA strategy, which is a graph refine-
ment method that can adaptively refine homogeneous regions
in heterogeneous graphs.
The GBA strategy mainly consists of two stages: 1 The
process of granular-ball formation . The process starts at a
coarse-grained level and progressively refines into smaller
granular-balls, capturing varying levels of homogeneous re-
gions. This hierarchical approach enables the model to rep-
resent both high-level relationships and fine-grained depen-
dencies, enhancing its ability to handle complex structures
of heterogeneous graphs. Following the cognitive law of
“global precedence” [30], the original graph is considered to
be the coarsest granular-ball. Then, initial granular-balls are

B. Performance of node classification and clustering (RQ1)
Node classification performance. Table III shows the
experimental results of node classification, and we can sum-
marize the following points:
• GBGCL performs well on most datasets, highlighting its
effectiveness and superiority in node classification tasks.
This result demonstrates that GBGCL is highly capable
of handling the complexities of different datasets, making
it a robust and reliable method for various heterogeneous
graph datasets. In addition, regarding the Chameleon and
Squirrel datasets, GBGCL and other baseline models did
not achieve optimal performance because the reference
[84] has theoretically demonstrated and experimentally
validated that GCN can achieve strong performance on
these two heterogeneous graph datasets.
• LR shows relatively low performance compared with
more advanced models, mainly because it cannot effec-
tively exploit the structural information of the graph.
Unlike GBGCL, which jointly considers topological and
feature information, LR relies primarily on node features
and thus fails to capture the underlying graph structure.
Moreover, GBGCL consistently outperforms other base-
lines that use X, A, and Y, demonstrating its stronger
ability to integrate heterogeneous sources of information
for node classification.
• Compared with other baselines, GBGCL more effec-
tively captures both structural and semantic information
in heterogeneous graphs, enabling it to model complex
node relationships and dependencies. By adaptively and
iteratively decomposing graphs into more homogeneous
regions, GBGCL refines homogeneous structures within
heterogeneous graphs.
We attribute this advantage to three factors: (1) GBGCL
adopts a multi-granularity granular-ball augmentation strategy
to adaptively identify regions with different topological scales,
thereby better capturing heterogeneity at multiple granularities.
(2) GBGCL effectively exploits the semantic information
embedded in original node features. (3) By using an MLP en-
coder to process structural and feature information separately,
GBGCL avoids the limited expressiveness caused by mixing
structure and feature information in GNN encoders under
heterogeneous settings. Together, these components improve
the adaptability and performance of GBGCL on heterogeneous
graph datasets.

VI. C ONCLUSION
In this paper, we propose a new multi-granularity graph con-
trastive learning framework via granular-ball on heterogeneous
graphs (GBGCL). The main innovation of this framework is
to introduce the granular-ball computing model into the graph
contrastive learning framework, and to propose a new granular-
ball augmentation strategy (GBA). This strategy adaptively
identifies multi-granularity homogeneous regions in heteroge-
neous graphs and effectively generates positive and negative
pairs. In addition, we also adopt a joint optimization module
to make full use of the structural and semantic information
of heterogeneous graphs, and further improve the quality of
node representation. Experimental results on several heteroge-
neous graph datasets show that GBGCL performs better than
comparison methods.
However, GBGCL also has certain limitations that require
further research. For example, although the proposed GBA
strategy has shown effectiveness on the evaluated dataset, its
computational overhead may become significant when applied
to graphs with millions of nodes or highly complex structures.
In these cases, more effective augmentation mechanisms may
be needed. Therefore, developing scalable and adaptive graph
augmentation strategies remains a valuable direction for future
work. This paper mainly conducts experimental verification on
static heterogeneous graph data, extending the framework to
dynamic graph structures, which is also a promising research
direction.
