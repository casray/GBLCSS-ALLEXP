\subsubsection{Sensitivity to construction and target parameters}\label{sec:4.3.4}

We examine how the parameters affect the formation of local groups, the number of nodes that can use local evidence, and the weight given to the most-voted class. Table~I.1 varies one parameter at a time on all five datasets under uniform 0.4 noise, using fixed pretrained representations and five paired seeds.

The ball budget and splitting threshold control how finely the nodes are grouped. On CoraFull, reducing \(B_{\max}\) from 64 to 32 lowered mean accuracy by 2.10 percentage points; increasing it to 128 raised accuracy by 1.57 points. The larger budget left Cora unchanged and slightly lowered accuracy on Coauthor-CS and Amazon-Photo. Raising \(\delta\) from 0.05 to 0.10 prevented splitting on the first four datasets, with accuracy losses of 3.04--13.15 points. Amazon-Photo still formed 64 balls and lost only 0.18 points. Lowering \(\delta\) to 0.01 changed accuracy by at most 0.04 points across the five datasets.

The voting threshold \(s_{\min}\) determines how many other labeled nodes are needed to use consensus. Raising it from 2 to 10 reduced the fraction of training nodes receiving a positive consensus weight to 56.53\% on Cora and 58.32\% on CiteSeer, accompanied by accuracy losses of 7.27 and 5.22 points. This fraction remained at least 89.96\% on the other three datasets, where accuracy changed by at most 0.38 points. The larger losses thus occurred on the datasets where the stricter threshold excluded substantially more nodes from consensus supervision.

The sharpening parameter \(\beta\) controls the weight given to a unique most-voted class within the consensus distribution. Increasing \(\beta\) from 0.5 to 1 raised mean accuracy on four datasets, most noticeably on CiteSeer (+1.74 points), and lowered it by 0.14 points on CoraFull. Changes to the minimum child-ball size \(n_{\min}\) also had dataset-dependent effects. Several configurations improved on the common default, with different settings yielding the highest mean on different datasets. Across the tested settings, the largest losses occurred when all nodes remained in one ball or substantially fewer nodes received consensus supervision.
