Confidence intervals are unadjusted, whereas the reported p-values are Holm-adjusted. Positive differences favor GB-LCSS; "Higher (seeds)" counts the seeds on which GB-LCSS has higher accuracy.

\subsection{Appendix D. Additional target-construction analyses}\label{appendix-d.-additional-target-construction-analyses}

Table 5 gives the uniform 0.4 vote and smoothing controls. Averaged over the five noise settings, the accuracy gain from excluding the current node's vote ranges from 0.08 to 1.03 percentage points across datasets, with a minimum setting-wise difference of −0.49 points.

With clean training labels and otherwise unchanged settings, the accuracy differences from MLP+CE are −0.24, +2.00, −0.38, −0.87, and −1.75 percentage points on Cora, CiteSeer, CoraFull, Coauthor-CS, and Amazon-Photo, respectively.

Ties among the most-voted classes occur in 123 of the 125 dataset-noise-seed runs and in 911 ball instances, affecting 12,788 of 259,000 training-node targets (4.94\%). When several classes tie for the most votes, we keep the full vote distribution instead of choosing one tied class. This prevents the result from depending on arbitrary class numbering.

\subsection{Appendix H. Analysis of local-consensus supervision}\label{appendix-h.-analysis-of-local-consensus-supervision}

This appendix proves Proposition 1 and derives the soft-target error identity in Section 3.4. All probabilities in Section H.1 condition on the graph, frozen representation, granular ball assignments, training-node set, and clean labels. Randomness comes from the label-corruption process, not from resampling graph nodes.

\subsubsection{H.1 Reliability of the local vote}\label{h.1-reliability-of-the-local-vote}

Fix a training node \(v\) in ball \(B_{j}\), with \(s_{v} = |T_{j, - v}| \geq s_{\min}\). The clean class proportions \(p_{v,c}^{clean}\) and the target-class margin \(\Delta_{v}\) are defined in Eq. (14). Each voting label independently stays unchanged with probability \(1 - \eta\) or changes to any particular incorrect class with probability \(\eta/(C - 1)\). For \(\gamma = 1 - C\eta/(C - 1)\), the expected empirical vote proportion is

\(\mathbb{E}\lbrack p_{j, - v,c}\rbrack = (1 - \eta)p_{v,c}^{clean} + \frac{\eta}{C - 1}(1 - p_{v,c}^{clean}) = \gamma p_{v,c}^{clean} + \frac{\eta}{C - 1}.\) (H.1)

Therefore, the expected vote difference between the current node\textquotesingle s true class and any competing class \(c \neq y_{v}\) is

\(\mathbb{E}\lbrack p_{j, - v,y_{v}} - p_{j, - v,c}\rbrack = \gamma(p_{v,y_{v}}^{clean} - p_{v,c}^{clean}) \geq \gamma\Delta_{v}.\) (H.2)

When \(\gamma > 0\), symmetric noise preserves the ordering of the clean class proportions in expectation. This does not require the true class to hold more than half of the votes: it requires a positive margin over every individual competing class.

For each competing class, define \(Z_{u,c} = \mathbb{I}({\widetilde{y}}_{u} = y_{v}) - \mathbb{I}({\widetilde{y}}_{u} = c)\) for \(u \in T_{j, - v}\). The variables \(Z_{u,c}\) are independent across voting nodes, lie in \(\lbrack - 1,1\rbrack\), and need not be identically distributed. Their average equals \(p_{j, - v,y_{v}} - p_{j, - v,c}\). Applying Hoeffding\textquotesingle s inequality {[}H1{]} gives

\(\begin{aligned}
\Pr(p_{j, - v,c} \geq p_{j, - v,y_{v}}) & \leq \exp\left\lbrack - \frac{s_{v}\gamma^{2}(p_{v,y_{v}}^{clean} - p_{v,c}^{clean})^{2}}{2} \right\rbrack \\
 & \leq \exp\left( - \frac{s_{v}\gamma^{2}\Delta_{v}^{2}}{2} \right).
\end{aligned}\) (H.3)

A union bound over the \(C - 1\) competing classes, capped at one, proves Eq. (15). A tie is included in the failure event. If the empirical winner is unique, Eq. (10) sharpens that winner without changing its identity; if several classes tie, it retains the empirical distribution. Thus the maximizing class set is unchanged for every \(\beta \in \lbrack 0,1\rbrack\), and the same probability bound applies to \(q_{j, - v}\).

The conditioning keeps the clean class composition fixed; it does not assume that clean graph labels are independent. Independent label corruption is the required probabilistic assumption. The positive-margin condition is node-specific: a ball\textquotesingle s dominant clean class need not be the class of every member. With a nonpositive margin, increasing the number of votes can reinforce evidence for a competing class. With few votes or a small positive margin, the bound may equal one and provide no informative numerical guarantee. The symmetric-noise ordering argument does not extend directly to pair or instance-dependent noise.

\subsubsection{H.2 Error of the consensus-weighted target}\label{h.2-error-of-the-consensus-weighted-target}

Fix any realization of the labels. For a node with sufficient voting support, use the abbreviations \(z = e_{{\widetilde{y}}_{v}}\), \(q = q_{j, - v}\), \(\alpha = \alpha_{j, - v}\), and \(e = e_{y_{v}}\). Expanding the square gives

\(\begin{aligned}
 \parallel t_{v} - e \parallel_{2}^{2} = & (1 - \alpha)^{2} \parallel z - e \parallel_{2}^{2} + \alpha^{2} \parallel q - e \parallel_{2}^{2} \\
 & + 2\alpha(1 - \alpha)\langle z - e,q - e\rangle.
\end{aligned}\) (H.4)

Substituting \(2\langle z - e,q - e\rangle = \parallel z - e \parallel_{2}^{2} + \parallel q - e \parallel_{2}^{2} - \parallel z - q \parallel_{2}^{2}\) yields Eq. (16). This identity is pointwise, so it remains valid when \(\alpha\) and \(q\) depend on the same local votes. For a node below the support threshold, Eq. (12) retains \(z\), giving zero change in target error.

If the observed label is correct, \(z = e\) and \(L_{v}(t_{v}) = \alpha^{2}L_{v}(q)\). If it is corrupted, \(L_{v}(z) = 2\), and the soft target can lower this error. Consequently, the change in the mean Brier score can be written exactly as

\(\begin{aligned}
BS(Z) - BS(T) = & \frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}_{L}|}\sum_{v \in \mathcal{N}}^{}\left( 2 - L_{v}(t_{v}) \right) \\
 & - \frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}_{L}|}\sum_{v \in \mathcal{K}}^{}L_{v}(t_{v}),
\end{aligned}\) (H.5)

where \(\mathcal{N} = \{ v \in \mathcal{V}_{L}:{\widetilde{y}}_{v} \neq y_{v}\}\) is the set of corrupted training labels and \(\mathcal{K} = \mathcal{V}_{L}\backslash\mathcal{N}\). The matrices \(Z\) and \(T\) contain the observed one-hot targets and the GB-LCSS targets, respectively; the Brier convention is the sum over classes, averaged over training nodes. Equation (H.5) separates improvement on corrupted labels from the perturbation of correct labels. A reduction in squared probability error need not change the most-probable class of a target.

A conditional sufficient condition follows under the independent symmetric-noise model. Hold the other observed training labels fixed, as well as the quantities conditioned on in Section H.1. Excluding the current node means that \(q\) and \(\alpha\) do not change when only its observed label is varied. Taking expectation over this node\textquotesingle s label corruption gives \(\mathbb{E}_{v}L_{v}(z) = 2\eta\). Equation (16) then implies

\(\mathbb{E}_{v}L_{v}(t_{v}) \leq (1 - \alpha)\, 2\eta + \alpha L_{v}(q).\) (H.6)

Hence \(L_{v}(q) < 2\eta\) and \(\alpha > 0\) are sufficient for lower conditional expected target error than the observed one-hot label. The condition concerns the quality of the local distribution, not merely its concentration. Proposition 1 controls which class receives the most local votes; it does not by itself establish this squared-error condition. The Brier comparisons in Section 4.3.1 evaluate target quality, while the classification experiments assess the performance of the trained MLP.

{[}H1{]} W. Hoeffding, Probability inequalities for sums of bounded random variables, J. Am. Stat. Assoc. 58 (1963) 13--30.

Random grouping also lowered Brier scores, yet yielded much lower classification accuracy (Table I.1). Target quality and downstream accuracy therefore provide complementary views of the supervision.

For Proposition 1, we also counted training nodes whose true class was more frequent than every competing class among the other training nodes in their ball, using clean labels. The proposition applies to these positive-margin nodes. The Brier decomposition in Table I.2 includes all training nodes.
